Downloads that finish.

Master Download Manager takes a file apart across many connections, keeps it resumable across crashes and restarts, and puts video, image galleries and torrents in the same list as everything else.

Everything in one list

Segmented downloads

One file, many connections. The engine splits large files, reopens what drops, and rejoins the pieces as they land.

Crash-safe resume

The part file lives beside the file itself and carries its own state, so a restart picks up where it stopped instead of from zero.

Browser capture

A native messaging host hands downloads from Chrome, Chromium, Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi and Firefox straight to the app.

Video and audio

Page downloads through yt-dlp, with lux, Streamlink and Ember as fallbacks for pages yt-dlp cannot read. Live streams via Streamlink.

Image galleries

Whole galleries through gallery-dl, with folders named after the site and the set.

BitTorrent

Magnets and .torrent files in the same list, with seeding limits, blocklists and proxy support.

Scheduler and queues

Start a queue at a time, limit it to a window, retry what fails, and keep a second queue for later.

Speed control

Per-download and global limits, so a large download does not take the whole line.

Archives

Extract .zip, .tar and .xz after the download; RAR and 7z through a 7-Zip you already have.

Checksums

Verify a finished file against MD5, SHA-1 or SHA-256 without leaving the app.

Remote control

Off by default. When you turn it on, paired phones and computers on your own network can add downloads over HTTPS.

85 languages

The interface, dates, sizes and speeds follow the language you choose.

Where it runs

Windows 10 and 11, 64-bit. Installer, or a portable folder that keeps its settings beside the program.
macOS Apple silicon and Intel builds.
Linux AppImage, .deb, .rpm and .tar.gz.

Releases are built from the source on GitHub and published with checksums.

Private by construction

There is no telemetry, no analytics, no account and no advertising. The app talks to the servers you give it, to its own release list when it checks for updates, and to the helper programs you choose to install. It sends nothing else anywhere.

A crash is written to a file in the app's own data folder and stays there; it goes nowhere unless you attach it to a report yourself.

The local automation API listens on 127.0.0.1 only, behind a token. Remote control binds the network interfaces you pick, with pairing, and is off until you enable it.

Free, and open

Master Download Manager is free software under the GNU GPL v3 or later. The source is published, the builds are made from it on GitHub, and the third-party assets and helper programs are listed with their licences.
